# Human test script

A 15-minute test of the site with real people. Five testers are enough to find most problems. Aim for a mix: one or two researchers in neuroscience or animal behaviour, a student, and someone with no science background. If possible include one person on a phone and one who uses the keyboard only or a screen reader.

Site: https://jordanxxlopez.github.io/animal-communication-neuroscience/

## How to run it

- Give the tester the link and nothing else. Do not explain the site.
- Ask them to think aloud. Do not help unless they are stuck for more than a minute; note where that happens.
- For each task write down: done or not done, roughly how long, and anything they said or hesitated over.
- Record the device and browser.

## First impression (1 minute, before any task)

Show the home page for 10 seconds, then hide it and ask:

1. What is this site about?
2. Who do you think made it, and would you trust the information on it?
3. What would you click first?

## Tasks

1. **Find a brain.** Open the brain atlas of the Norway rat and find the medial superior olive. Show it in 3D and in the sections.
2. **Share a view.** Rotate the 3D brain to a view you like and copy a link to it. Open the link in a new tab. Is it the same view?
3. **Listen.** Find a recording of rat ultrasonic calls and play it. What does the page say about the playback speed? Who recorded it and under what licence?
4. **Read a figure.** On the zebra finch page, go to "Neurons that listen". What do the white line and the coloured line show? What are the units on each axis?
5. **Check a claim.** In Research, open the honeybee waggle-dance analysis. Did the reanalysis agree with the claim? What was not tested?
6. **Find the source.** For the marmoset atlas, find the paper to cite and the licence.
7. **Compare species.** Which species in the collection have no brain atlas, and how does the site say so?
8. **Change the display.** Switch to dark theme and larger text, then switch back.
9. **Report a mistake.** Suppose a citation is wrong. Where would you report it?

## Phone-only checks

- Can the page be scrolled past a 3D brain with one finger?
- Is the menu fully visible and usable?
- Does any page scroll sideways?

## Keyboard and screen-reader checks

- Reach the main content with the "Skip to content" link.
- Choose a region in an atlas region list using Tab and the arrow keys.
- Rotate a 3D brain with the arrow keys.
- Play a recording without a mouse.
- Does the screen reader announce the selected region and the selected recording?

## Closing questions

1. What was the most confusing moment?
2. Was any text unclear, too technical, or too simple?
3. Did anything look unfinished or untrustworthy?
4. Would you use this site again? For what?
